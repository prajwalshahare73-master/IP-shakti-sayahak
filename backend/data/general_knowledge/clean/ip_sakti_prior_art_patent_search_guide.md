1. What is Prior Art?
What is prior art?
Simple Answer
Prior art is all information made available to the public before the filing/priority date of a patent application that
is relevant to assessing novelty and inventive step.
Prior art includes earlier patents, published applications, journal articles, products, public uses, and traditional
knowledge (including TKDL). If an invention is fully disclosed in prior art, it lacks novelty.
 Used to test novelty (same invention already known?) and inventive step (obvious to skilled person?).
 TKDL is treated as prior art for traditional medicinal knowledge.
 Non■patent literature and public uses can also be prior art.
 Prior art is global; not limited to India or patents only.
Relevant Act / Rule / Guideline
Patents Act 1970 – Sections 2(1)(l), 13, 25 (opposition grounds).
Responsible Authority
Indian Patent Office; Courts.
Common Mistakes
 Considering only Indian patents as prior art.
 Ignoring non■patent literature (journals, textbooks, traditional texts).
 Assuming unpublished or secret knowledge is prior art (generally not).
An Ayurvedic formulation described in a classical text or TKDL is prior art against a later patent claim on the same
formulation.
 Source title: Patents Act 1970; IP India examination guidelines
 Issuing authority: IP India / DPIIT
 URL: https://www.ipindia.gov.in/resource/patents-resources-act
Prior art analysis is fact■specific and technical; this is a conceptual overview.

2. Why Prior Art Matters for Patents
Why does prior art matter for patents?
Simple Answer
Prior art determines whether an invention is new and non■obvious. Examiners and opponents use prior art to
reject or revoke patents that claim already known or obvious subject matter.
For applicants, understanding prior art helps draft better claims, avoid wasted filing costs, and anticipate
objections.
 Novelty: invention must not be fully disclosed in a single prior art document/use.
 Inventive step: invention must not be obvious in light of prior art.
 Prior art can be used in examination, oppositions, and revocation proceedings.
 Good prior art search reduces risk of rejection/invalidity.
Relevant Act / Rule / Guideline
Patents Act 1970 – Sections 2(1)(j), (ja), 13, 25, 64.
Responsible Authority
Indian Patent Office; Courts.
Common Mistakes
 Filing without any prior art search.
 Relying only on keyword search without classification.
 Ignoring traditional knowledge as potential prior art.
A startup files a patent on an herbal extract without searching classical texts; later, an opponent cites TKDL references,
leading to refusal/revocation.
 Source title: Patents Act 1970; IP India examination guidelines
 Issuing authority: IP India / DPIIT
 URL: https://www.ipindia.gov.in/resource/patents-resources-act
This is a general explanation; legal standards for novelty/inventive step are nuanced.

3. Types of Prior Art (Patents and Non■Patent)
What are the main types of prior art?
Simple Answer
Prior art includes patent documents (published applications, granted patents) and non■patent literature
(journals, books, conference papers, standards, theses, websites, traditional texts, TKDL).
Both types are important. Many inventions are blocked by non■patent prior art, especially in fields like
Ayurveda where traditional knowledge is key.
 Patent prior art: published patent applications, granted patents, PCT applications.
 Non■patent prior art: scientific articles, textbooks, conference proceedings, product manuals, websites.
 Traditional knowledge: classical texts, TKDL, oral traditions (if documented/public).
 Public use/sale: products sold or used publicly before filing date.
Key Tools / Databases
 Patents: IP India patent search, WIPO PATENTSCOPE, EPO Espacenet, USPTO PatentFT.
 Non■patent: Google Scholar, PubMed, IEEE Xplore (for tech), classical text databases, TKDL (via examiners).
Common Mistakes
 Searching only patents and ignoring journals/books.
 Ignoring traditional texts for Ayurveda/biotech inventions.
 Assuming only English■language documents matter.
For an Ayurvedic pain■relief formulation, prior art may include classical Sanskrit texts, TKDL entries, modern journal
articles on herbal analgesics, and existing patents on similar compositions.
 Source title: IP India; WIPO; EPO; USPTO; TKDL documentation
 Issuing authority: IP India; WIPO; EPO; USPTO; CSIR■TKDL
 URL: https://ipindia.gov.in/ ; https://patentscope.wipo.int/ ; https://worldwide.espacenet.com/ ; https://patft.uspto.gov/
This is a general classification; specific cases may involve other types of disclosures.

4. Patent Search Databases (Official and Free)
Which patent search databases should I use?
Simple Answer
Use a combination of official and free databases: IP India for Indian patents, WIPO PATENTSCOPE for
PCT/international, EPO Espacenet for global coverage, and USPTO for US patents. Commercial databases
exist but are not required for basic searches.
Each database has strengths: IP India for local filings, PATENTSCOPE for PCT, Espacenet for classification
and family data, USPTO for detailed US prior art.
 IP India: Indian published applications and granted patents.
 WIPO PATENTSCOPE: PCT applications and many national collections.
 EPO Espacenet: Global patent data with strong classification tools.
 USPTO: US patents/applications with full■text search.
Key Tools / Databases
 IP India Patent Search: https://ipindia.gov.in/ (Patents e■register/search).
 WIPO PATENTSCOPE: https://patentscope.wipo.int/.
 EPO Espacenet: https://worldwide.espacenet.com/.
 USPTO PatentFT: https://patft.uspto.gov/.
Common Mistakes
 Relying on a single database only.
 Using only keyword search without classification.
 Not checking family members and citations.
Searching an Ayurvedic extract patent: start with IP India for Indian filings, then expand to PATENTSCOPE/Espacenet for
international family and citations.
 Source title: IP India; WIPO; EPO; USPTO
 Issuing authority: IP India; WIPO; EPO; USPTO
 URL: https://ipindia.gov.in/ ; https://patentscope.wipo.int/ ; https://worldwide.espacenet.com/ ; https://patft.uspto.gov/
Database coverage and interfaces change; always check latest help pages.

5. Classification Systems (IPC / CPC)
What are IPC and CPC classifications and why do they matter?
Simple Answer
IPC (International Patent Classification) and CPC (Cooperative Patent Classification) are hierarchical
systems that categorize patents by technical field. They help find relevant prior art even when keywords
differ.
Using classifications improves search precision, especially for technical inventions where terminology varies.
 IPC: Sections A–H (e.g., A61K for medical preparations).
 CPC: Extension of IPC with finer granularity (e.g., A61K36/00 for plant extracts).
 Classification search complements keyword search.
 Important for Ayurveda/herbal inventions (A61K, C11B, C07D, etc.).
Step-by-Step Process
1. Identify technical field of invention (e.g., herbal medicine, extraction process).
2. Find relevant IPC/CPC classes using classification search tools.
3. Search patents within those classes for prior art.
4. Combine with keyword searches for comprehensive coverage.
Key Tools / Databases
 EPO CPC Scheme: https://www.epo.org/searching-for- patents/classification/cpc.html.
 WIPO IPC: https://www.wipo.int/classifications/ipc/.
 Espacenet classification search.
Common Mistakes
 Ignoring classification and relying only on keywords.
 Using outdated classification versions.
 Not exploring subgroups for finer search.
An Ayurvedic formulation for diabetes may be classified under A61K36/00 (plant extracts) with subgroups for specific
plants or conditions.
 Source title: WIPO IPC; EPO CPC; Espacenet
 Issuing authority: WIPO; EPO
 URL: https://www.wipo.int/classifications/ipc/ ; https://www.epo.org/searching-for-patents/classification/cpc.html ;
https://worldwide.espacenet.com/

Classification assignment is done by examiners; applicants can suggest but not fix classification.

6. Keyword Search Strategies
How do I build effective keyword searches for patents?
Simple Answer
Effective keyword searches use synonyms, variants, truncation, and Boolean operators (AND, OR, NOT) to
capture different ways the same concept can be described.
For Ayurveda, include Sanskrit/Hindi names, Latin botanical names, and common English terms.
 Use synonyms: e.g., 'Ashwagandha' OR 'Withania somnifera' OR 'Indian ginseng'.
 Use truncation/wildcards: e.g., 'extract*' for extract, extracts, extraction.
 Combine concepts: e.g., '(Ashwagandha OR Withania) AND (extract OR composition) AND (anti■inflammatory OR
arthritis)'.
 Search titles, abstracts, claims, and full text where available.
Step-by-Step Process
1. List key technical concepts (herb, condition, process, effect).
2. For each concept, list synonyms, scientific names, and variants.
3. Build Boolean search strings combining concepts.
4. Test and refine searches based on results.
Key Tools / Databases
 IP India patent search (keyword fields).
 PATENTSCOPE, Espacenet, USPTO with advanced search.
 Google Patents for quick keyword checks.
Common Mistakes
 Using only one term per concept (missing synonyms).
 Overly narrow searches that miss relevant documents.
 Not adjusting for different languages/nomenclature.
Searching for a turmeric■based wound healing formulation: use '(turmeric OR Curcuma longa) AND (wound OR healing
OR ulcer) AND (ointment OR gel OR composition)'.
 Source title: IP India; WIPO; EPO; USPTO search guides
 Issuing authority: IP India; WIPO; EPO; USPTO
 URL: https://ipindia.gov.in/ ; https://patentscope.wipo.int/ ; https://worldwide.espacenet.com/ ; https://patft.uspto.gov/
Keyword search alone is not sufficient; combine with classification.

7. Step-by-Step Prior Art Search Process
What is a step-by-step prior art search process?
Simple Answer
A systematic prior art search combines keyword and classification searches across multiple databases,
reviews key documents, and documents the strategy for future reference.
This process helps ensure thoroughness and reproducibility.
 Start with a clear definition of the invention's technical features.
 Use both keyword and classification approaches.
 Search multiple databases (IP India, PATENTSCOPE, Espacenet, etc.).
 Record search strings, classes, and key documents found.
Step-by-Step Process
1. Define technical problem and key features of the invention.
2. Identify keywords and synonyms (including local/Latin names).
3. Identify relevant IPC/CPC classes.
4. Search IP India for Indian prior art.
5. Search PATENTSCOPE/Espacenet for international prior art.
6. Review top results for similarity to your claims.
7. Note relevant documents for novelty/inventive step analysis.
8. Document search strategy and results for records.
Key Tools / Databases
 IP India patent search.
 WIPO PATENTSCOPE.
 EPO Espacenet.
 Google Patents (for quick checks).
Common Mistakes
 Stopping after one database or one search string.
 Not recording search strategy (hard to reproduce).
 Ignoring non■patent literature for key fields.
For a new herbal anti■diabetic composition, searcher defines features (herbs, formulation, use), builds keyword strings,
identifies A61K classes, searches IP India + PATENTSCOPE, and documents top 10 relevant patents/articles.
 Source title: IP India; WIPO; EPO search guidelines
 Issuing authority: IP India; WIPO; EPO
 URL: https://ipindia.gov.in/ ; https://patentscope.wipo.int/ ; https://worldwide.espacenet.com/

This is a general process; professional searches may be more exhaustive.

8. Traditional Knowledge and TKDL in Prior Art
How does traditional knowledge and TKDL fit into prior art?
Simple Answer
Traditional knowledge (TK) documented in classical texts, TKDL, and other public sources is prior art for
patent examination. TKDL is specifically used by patent examiners to find traditional medicinal knowledge
during search.
For Ayurveda inventions, TK and TKDL are critical prior art sources that can block patents on known
formulations.
 TKDL contains translations/classifications of Ayurveda, Unani, Siddha, Sowa Rigpa, Yoga formulations.
 Examiners at 18+ patent offices access TKDL under confidentiality agreements.
 Classical texts (Charaka, Sushruta, etc.) are prior art if publicly available.
 TK can be cited in oppositions/revocations against wrongful patents.
Key Tools / Databases
 TKDL (accessed by examiners; not publicly searchable in full).
 Classical text databases and publications.
 Non■patent literature on traditional medicine.
Common Mistakes
 Assuming TK is not prior art because it is 'traditional'.
 Ignoring TKDL when assessing Ayurvedic inventions.
 Not checking classical texts for formulation disclosures.
A patent claim on a classical Ashwagandha formulation is rejected after examiner finds matching TKDL entry showing
prior existence.
 Source title: CSIR■TKDL; PIB; Patents Act
 Issuing authority: CSIR; PIB; IP India
 URL: https://www.csir.res.in/ ; https://www.pib.gov.in/
TKDL is not fully public; applicants must rely on examiners and their own classical text searches.

9. Common Mistakes in Patent Search
What are common mistakes in patent/prior art search?
Simple Answer
Common mistakes include using only keywords, searching one database, ignoring classifications, missing
synonyms, and not documenting the search. These lead to incomplete results and false confidence.
Avoiding these mistakes improves search quality and reduces risk of missing critical prior art.
 Keyword■only search misses documents with different terminology.
 Single■database search misses coverage gaps.
 Ignoring classifications reduces precision.
 Not recording strategy makes it hard to reproduce/improve.
Common Mistakes
 Searching only English terms for Indian herbs (missing Latin/Sanskrit).
 Not checking citations/backward-forward references.
 Assuming no results means no prior art (may be search issue).
 Ignoring non■patent literature in key fields.
Searcher uses only 'Ashwagandha' in English, misses 'Withania somnifera' patents; later, opponent finds relevant
documents using scientific name.
 Source title: IP India; WIPO; EPO search guidelines
 Issuing authority: IP India; WIPO; EPO
 URL: https://ipindia.gov.in/ ; https://patentscope.wipo.int/ ; https://worldwide.espacenet.com/
This is a general list; specific mistakes depend on field and searcher experience.
