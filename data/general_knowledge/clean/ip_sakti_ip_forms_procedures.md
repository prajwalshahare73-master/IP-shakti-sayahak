1. Patent Filing Procedure (India)
Patent Filing Procedure (India)
Purpose
To obtain a patent in India, an applicant must file a patent application with the Indian Patent Office, disclosing
the invention in a specification and paying the prescribed fees. This starts the process that may lead to grant
of a patent.
When to Use
Use this procedure when you have an invention (product or process) that you believe is new, involves an
inventive step, and is industrially applicable, and you want to seek patent protection in India.
Step-by-Step Process
1. Confirm that the subject matter is patentable (not excluded under Section 3 of Patents Act).
2. Conduct a prior■art search (patents, publications, TKDL context for traditional knowledge).
3. Decide whether to file provisional (to secure early filing date) or complete specification.
4. Prepare Form 1 (application) and Form 2 (specification) with title, abstract, description, claims, drawings (if any).
5. File e■application on IP India portal (Patents) and pay filing fee (First Schedule, Patents Rules).
6. If applicable, file Form 3 (statement & undertaking regarding foreign filings) within prescribed time.
7. Application is published at 18 months from filing/priority (or earlier if Form 9 is filed).
Key Forms
 Form 1: Application for Patent
 Form 2: Provisional/Complete Specification
 Form 3: Statement & Undertaking (foreign filings)
 Form 9: Request for Early Publication (optional)
 Form 28: Declaration as to Small Entity / Startup (for reduced fees, if applicable)
Key Fees (official, indicative)
 Filing (Form 1+2, ≤30 pages, ≤10 claims, e■filing): ■1,600 (reduced: natural person/startup/small/edu) / ■8,000 (large).
 Each extra page (>30): ■160 (reduced) / ■800 (large).
 Each extra claim (>10): ■320 (reduced) / ■1,600 (large).
 Early Publication (Form 9): ■2,500 (reduced) / ■12,500 (large) [verify on IP India].
Responsible Authority
Indian Patent Office (CGPDTM), under DPIIT.

Common Mistakes
 Filing without adequate prior■art search.

 Disclosing invention publicly before filing (can destroy novelty).
 Incorrect applicant/inventor details.
 Not filing Form 3 within prescribed time when foreign applications exist.
Practical Notes
For inventions related to Ayurveda/traditional knowledge, carefully assess whether the formulation is already described in
classical texts or TKDL; this can affect novelty and patentability.
Limitations
This is a general procedure; specific technical/legal drafting and claim strategy require a qualified patent professional.

2. Patent Examination & Grant Procedure
Patent Examination & Grant Procedure
Purpose
After filing, a patent application must be examined by the Patent Office. The applicant must request
examination, respond to objections, and comply with requirements before grant.
When to Use
Use this procedure after filing a patent application when you want the application to be examined and
potentially granted.
Step-by-Step Process
1. File Request for Examination (Form 18) within prescribed time (generally 48 months from filing/priority).
2. Optionally, file Form 18A for Expedited Examination (if eligible and desired).
3. Patent examiner issues First Examination Report (FER) citing objections (novelty, inventive step, clarity, etc.).
4. Respond to FER with arguments, amendments, and/or evidence within prescribed time (typically 6 months, extendable
by 3 months).
5. Attend hearing if required; address remaining objections.
6. On acceptance, pay grant & publication fees (if applicable) and receive patent certificate.
7. Pay renewal fees from 3rd year onwards to keep patent in force.
Key Forms
 Form 18: Request for Examination
 Form 18A: Request for Expedited Examination
 Form 13: Amendment of Application/Specification (if needed)
Key Fees (official, indicative)
 Request for Examination (Form 18, e■filing): ■4,000 (reduced) / ■20,000 (large).
 Expedited Examination (Form 18A): ■8,000 (reduced) / ■60,000 (large) [verify on IP India].
 Renewals: from 3rd year onwards, increasing slabs (e.g., 3rd year: ■800 / ■4,000).
Responsible Authority
Indian Patent Office (CGPDTM), under DPIIT.
Source Details
 Source title: The Patents Act, 1970; Patents Rules 2003; IP India – Patents resources
 Issuing authority: IP India / DPIIT
 URL: https://www.ipindia.gov.in/resource/patents-resources-act ; https://ipindia.gov.in/
 Verification date: 10 Sep 2026
Verification Date

Common Mistakes
 Missing examination request deadline (application deemed withdrawn).
 Inadequate response to FER (leading to refusal).
 Not paying renewal fees on time (patent ceases).

Practical Notes
Examination timelines vary by technology area and office workload; expedited examination can reduce time but costs
more.
Limitations
This is a general procedure; complex cases (e.g., biotech, software■related, traditional knowledge) need specialized
handling.

3. Trademark Filing & Registration Procedure
Trademark Filing & Registration Procedure
Purpose
To register a trademark in India, an applicant must file an application with the Trade Marks Registry,
specifying the mark, class(es) of goods/services, and paying the prescribed fee. This starts the process that
may lead to registration.
When to Use
Use this procedure when you have a brand name, logo, label, or other sign that you want to protect as a
trademark for your goods/services in India.
Step-by-Step Process
1. Conduct a trademark search on tmrsearch.ipindia.gov.in for identical/similar marks in relevant classes.
2. Identify appropriate Nice class(es) for your goods/services.
3. Prepare application details: mark representation, applicant details, class(es), description of goods/services, date of first
use (if any).
4. File Form TM■A (e■filing) on IP India portal (Trade Marks) and pay fee (First Schedule, Trade Marks Rules).
5. Examination: Registrar issues examination report (acceptance/refusal/objections).
6. Respond to objections within prescribed time (typically 1 month).
7. On acceptance, mark is published in Trade Marks Journal.
8. Opposition window: 4 months from publication.
9. If no opposition/successful defence, registration certificate is issued.
Key Forms
 TM■A: Application for Registration of Trademark
 TM■M: Request for Expedited Examination (optional)
 TM■16: Response to Examination Report
 TM■R: Request for Renewal (after 10 years)
Key Fees (official, indicative)
 TM■A (e■filing, per class): ■4,500 (Individual/Startup/Small Enterprise) / ■9,000 (Others).
 TM■A (physical, per class): ■5,000 / ■10,000.
 TM■M (expedited, per class): ■20,000 (reduced) / ■40,000 (others) [verify current fee on IP India].
Responsible Authority
Trade Marks Registry (CGPDTM), IP India.
Source Details
 Source title: The Trade Marks Act, 1999; Trade Marks Rules, 2017; IP India – Trade Marks resources
 Issuing authority: IP India / DPIIT
 URL: https://ipindia.gov.in/
 Verification date: 10 Sep 2026

Common Mistakes

 Filing in wrong class(es).
 Choosing a descriptive/generic mark without evidence of distinctiveness.
 Ignoring earlier conflicting marks in search.
 Missing response deadline to examination report.
Practical Notes
For Ayurvedic brands, consider filing in Class 5 (pharmaceuticals) and/or Class 3 (cosmetics) depending on product
claims and positioning.
Limitations
This is a general procedure; complex oppositions or distinctiveness issues need legal strategy.

4. Trademark Opposition & Rectification (Overview)
Trademark Opposition & Rectification (Overview)
Purpose
After publication in the Trade Marks Journal, third parties can oppose registration within 4 months.
Rectification proceedings allow correction/removal of registered marks on specified grounds.
When to Use
Use opposition when you believe a published mark conflicts with your earlier rights or violates the Act. Use
rectification to correct/remove an existing registration.
Step-by-Step Process
1. Opposition: File notice of opposition (TM■O) within 4 months of publication in Trade Marks Journal.
2. Applicant files counter■statement; evidence rounds follow.
3. Hearing before Registrar; decision on opposition.
4. Rectification: File petition (TM■O) before Registrar or IP Appellate Board on grounds such as non■use, wrongful
registration, etc.
5. Evidence and hearing; order on rectification.
Key Forms
 TM■O: Notice of Opposition / Rectification Petition
 TM■16: Counter■Statement (by applicant)
Key Fees (official, indicative)
 TM■O (opposition/rectification, per class): approx. ■2,700 (verify current fee on IP India).
Responsible Authority
Trade Marks Registry (CGPDTM); IP Appellate Board (for certain appeals).
Source Details
 Source title: The Trade Marks Act, 1999; Trade Marks Rules, 2017; IP India – Trade Marks resources
 Issuing authority: IP India / DPIIT
 URL: https://ipindia.gov.in/
 Verification date: 10 Sep 2026
Verification Date
Verification Date: 10 Sep 2026
Common Mistakes
 Missing 4■month opposition deadline.
 Filing opposition without solid grounds/evidence.
 Not filing counter■statement in time (mark may be deemed abandoned).
Practical Notes
Oppositions are common for similar marks in same/related classes; early search and monitoring of Trade Marks Journal
is important.
Limitations

This is a high■level overview; opposition/rectification strategy requires detailed legal analysis.

5. GI Registration Procedure
GI Registration Procedure
Purpose
To register a Geographical Indication (GI) in India, an applicant (usually a producer association or competent
authority) must file an application showing the link between product and geographical origin, and pay the
prescribed fee.
When to Use
Use this procedure when you represent producers of a product whose quality, reputation, or characteristics
are essentially due to its geographical origin.
Step-by-Step Process
1. Define geographical area and product characteristics.
2. Gather evidence of link between origin and quality/reputation (historical, technical, market).
3. Prepare GI application (Form GI■1) with details of territory, product, and producers.
4. File with GI Registry (IP India) and pay fee (First Schedule, GI Rules).
5. Examination: Registrar issues examination report.
6. On acceptance, application is published in GI Journal.
7. Opposition window (typically 3 months).
8. If no opposition/successful defence, GI is registered; authorised users can be added (Form GI■3).
Key Forms
 GI■1: Application for Registration of GI
 GI■2: Renewal of GI Registration
 GI■3: Application for Authorised User
 GI■4: Notice of Opposition
Key Fees (official, indicative)
 GI registration (one class): ■1,000 (as per GI (Amendment) Rules 2025).
 GI renewal: ■500.
 Additional protection (Section 22(2)): ■12,000.
 Restoration: ■1,000 (+ renewal if applicable).
Responsible Authority
GI Registry (CGPDTM), IP India.
Source Details
 Source title: GI Act 1999; GI Rules 2002 as amended 2025 (Gazette Notification); IP India – GI resources
 Issuing authority: DPIIT / GI Registry
 URL: https://ipindia.gov.in/writereaddata/Portal/Images/pdf/GAZETTE_NOTIFICATI
ON_AMENDED_GI_RULES_2025.pdf ; https://ipindia.gov.in/
 Verification date: 10 Sep 2026
Verification Date

Common Mistakes

 Weak evidence of link between origin and quality/reputation.
 Filing by a single company without representing producers/region.
 Ignoring opposition risks from other producers.
Practical Notes
GI is especially relevant for regional Ayurvedic/herbal products with established reputation (e.g., specific regional
formulations).
Limitations
This is a general procedure; GI applications often require detailed historical/technical documentation.

6. Copyright Registration Procedure
Copyright Registration Procedure
Purpose
To register a copyright in India, an applicant must file Form XIV with the Copyright Office, providing details of
the work and paying the prescribed fee. Registration is optional but provides strong evidence.
When to Use
Use this procedure when you want to register copyright in a literary, dramatic, musical, artistic work, film,
sound recording, or software.
Step-by-Step Process
1. Create account on copyright.gov.in.
2. Prepare work details: title, author, owner, language, category, publication details (if any).
3. File Form XIV online, upload soft copy of work, and pay fee (Second Schedule, Copyright Rules).
4. Wait 30 days for objections.
5. If no objection, application is examined; discrepancies may be raised.
6. On satisfaction, registration certificate is issued.
Key Forms
 Form XIV: Application for Registration of Copyright
 Form XV: Application for Registration of Change in Particulars
Key Fees (official, indicative)
 Literary/Dramatic/Musical/Artistic work: ■500 per application.
 Artistic work used in relation to goods/services (e.g., logo as trademark): ■2,000.
 Cinematograph film: ■5,000.
 Sound recording: ■2,000.
 Software (as literary work): ■500.
Responsible Authority
Copyright Office, Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade (DPIIT).
Source Details
 Source title: The Copyright Act, 1957; Copyright Rules, 2013 (Second Schedule); Copyright Office
 Issuing authority: Copyright Office / DPIIT
 URL: https://copyright.gov.in/
 Verification date: 10 Sep 2026
Verification Date
Verification Date: 10 Sep 2026
Common Mistakes
 Assuming registration is mandatory for protection (it is not).
 Filing for ideas instead of expression.
 Incorrect work category leading to fee mismatch.
 Not providing clear authorship/ownership details.

Practical Notes
For Ayurvedic companies, brochures, labels, website content, and training manuals can be copyrighted as literary/artistic
works.
Limitations
Registration does not create the right; it evidences existing copyright.

7. Design Registration Procedure
Design Registration Procedure
Purpose
To register a design in India, an applicant must file Form 1 with the Designs Office, providing representations
of the design and paying the prescribed fee. Registration protects the visual appearance of the article.
When to Use
Use this procedure when you have a new/original design for the shape, configuration, pattern, or
ornamentation of an article.
Step-by-Step Process
1. Prepare clear drawings/photos of the design (multiple views: front, back, sides, top, bottom, perspective).
2. Ensure design is new/original and not dictated solely by function.
3. File Form 1 (e■filing) on IP India portal (Designs) with representations and fee (First Schedule, Designs Rules).
4. Examination: Registrar issues examination report.
5. Respond to objections (if any) within prescribed time.
6. On acceptance, design is registered; certificate issued.
7. Renew after 10 years (if desired) via Form 3.
Key Forms
 Form 1: Application for Registration of Design
 Form 2: Request for Extension of Time
 Form 3: Request for Renewal of Design
 Form 4: Petition for Restoration of Lapsed Design
Key Fees (official, indicative)
 Design registration (Form 1): ■1,000 (natural person/startup/small) / ■4,000 (large).
 Renewal (Form 3): ■2,000 (reduced) / ■8,000 (large).
 Restoration (Form 4): ■1,000 (reduced) / ■4,000 (large).
Responsible Authority
Designs Office (CGPDTM), IP India.
Source Details
 Source title: The Designs Act, 2000; Designs Rules, 2001 as amended 2021; IP India – Designs resources
 Issuing authority: CGPDTM / DPIIT
 URL: https://ipindia.gov.in/
 Verification date: 10 Sep 2026
Verification Date
Verification Date: 10 Sep 2026
Common Mistakes
 Trying to protect functional features via design.
 Poor quality drawings/views leading to objections.
 Missing renewal deadline.

Practical Notes
Design registration is useful for distinctive packaging, bottle shapes, and product■■ for consumer Ayurvedic products.
Limitations
Design registration does not protect functional/technical features.
