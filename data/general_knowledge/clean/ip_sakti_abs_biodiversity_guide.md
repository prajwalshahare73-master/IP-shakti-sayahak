1. What is ABS (Access and Benefit Sharing)?
What is ABS (Access and Benefit Sharing)?
Simple Answer
ABS means the fair and equitable sharing of benefits arising from the use of biological resources and
associated traditional knowledge, as required under the Biological Diversity Act, 2002 (as amended 2023)
and Rules.
If you access Indian biological resources (or associated TK) for research, commercial use, or IPR filing, you
may need NBA approval and to share benefits (monetary/non■monetary) with providers/local communities.
 Applies to biological resources and associated TK accessed in India.
 NBA (National Biodiversity Authority) grants approvals for certain categories; State Biodiversity Boards handle others.
 IPR applicants must disclose source and comply with ABS before grant in many cases.
 Benefits can be monetary (royalties, milestone payments) or non■monetary (technology transfer, capacity building).
Biological Diversity Act, 2002 (as amended 2023); Biological Diversity Rules, 2004 (as amended); NBA notifications.
National Biodiversity Authority (NBA); State Biodiversity Boards.
Common Mistakes
 Accessing biological resources without checking ABS obligations.
 Filing IPR without disclosing source of biological resource/TK.
 Assuming cultivated herbs are always exempt (depends on use and category).
A company isolates an active compound from an Indian herb and files a patent. ABS laws may require NBA approval and
benefit sharing with local communities/providers.
 Source title: NBA portal; MoEFCC; Biological Diversity Act/Rules
 Issuing authority: NBA; Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change
 URL: https://nbaindia.nic.in/
ABS obligations depend on resource type, user category, and state rules; seek specific advice for complex cases.

2. Biological Diversity Act, 2002 (as amended 2023) – Overview
What is the Biological Diversity Act, 2002 (as amended 2023)?
Simple Answer
The Biological Diversity Act, 2002 (as amended 2023) implements India's commitments under the
Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD) and Nagoya Protocol. It regulates access to biological resources
and associated TK and ensures fair benefit sharing.
The Act establishes NBA at the national level and State Biodiversity Boards at the state level. It sets rules for
who needs approval, how benefits are shared, and how IPR applications must comply.
 Covers biological resources (plants, animals, microbes, etc.) and associated TK.
 Distinguishes between Indian and foreign entities, and between different user categories.
 Requires ABS agreements for commercial use/research/IPR in many cases.
 2023 amendments introduced changes in exemptions, procedures, and penalties; check latest text.
Biological Diversity Act, 2002 (as amended 2023); Biological Diversity Rules, 2004 (as amended).
National Biodiversity Authority (NBA); State Biodiversity Boards; MoEFCC.
Common Mistakes
 Relying on pre■2023 summaries without checking amended provisions.
 Assuming all traditional knowledge is free to use without ABS.
 Ignoring state■level requirements and Biodiversity Management Committees.
An Ayurvedic company using Indian herbs for new proprietary formulations must check if its activities trigger ABS
obligations under the amended Act and Rules.
 Source title: Biological Diversity Act 2002 (as amended 2023); NBA portal
 Issuing authority: Parliament of India; NBA; MoEFCC
 URL: https://nbaindia.nic.in/ ; https://moef.gov.in/
This is a high■level overview; specific obligations depend on exact activities and resources.

3. Who Needs NBA / State Board Approval?
Who needs NBA or State Biodiversity Board approval?
Simple Answer
Approval requirements depend on who is accessing the resource (Indian/foreign), the purpose
(research/commercial/IPR), and the source (wild/cultivated/institution). In general, foreign entities and certain
commercial users need NBA approval; many Indian users deal with State Boards.
The Act and Rules specify categories that need prior approval and those that are exempt or simplified.
 Foreign entities accessing Indian biological resources/TK generally need NBA approval.
 Indian entities engaged in commercial utilization or certain research may need State Board/NBA approval depending on
category.
 IPR applicants (including Indians) often must disclose source and show ABS compliance.
 Some cultivated resources and certain categories may have simplified or exempt status (check latest Rules).
Step-by-Step Process
1. Identify if your activity involves Indian biological resources/associated TK.
2. Determine your user category (Indian/foreign; research/commercial/IPR).
3. Check if NBA or State Board approval is needed (based on applicant type and purpose).
4. File required ABS application (e.g., Form■I) and obtain approval.
5. Comply with benefit■sharing terms (agreements, payments, etc.).
Biological Diversity Act, 2002 (as amended 2023); Biological Diversity Rules, 2004 (as amended).
National Biodiversity Authority (NBA); State Biodiversity Boards.
Forms
 Form■I: Application for access to biological resources/associated TK (as per Rules).
 Other forms for IPR disclosure, exemptions, etc., as notified.
Fees
 Fees and benefit sharing terms vary by case; often determined via ABS agreement.
 Not verified from current official source for all categories; check NBA/State Board portal.
Common Mistakes
 Assuming no approval is needed because resources are cultivated.
 Filing IPR without checking ABS disclosure requirements.
 Not engaging with State Board/BMC where required.
A foreign university collaborating with an Indian lab on Indian medicinal plants needs NBA approval for access and must
comply with ABS terms.

4. ABS and IPR (Patents, etc.) – Key Requirements
How does ABS relate to IPR (patents, etc.)?
Simple Answer
If your invention uses Indian biological resources (e.g., herbs) or associated traditional knowledge, ABS laws
may require NBA/State Board approval and benefit sharing before filing or grant of IPR. Non■compliance can
affect patent validity and lead to penalties.
IPR applicants must often disclose source of biological resource/TK and provide ABS approval evidence
when required.
 Biological Diversity Act requires ABS compliance for certain users and IPR applicants.
 IPR applications may be scrutinized for source disclosure and ABS compliance.
 Benefits can be monetary (royalties) or non■monetary (technology transfer, capacity building).
 Non■compliance can lead to opposition, revocation, or penalties under the Act.
Step-by-Step Process
1. Identify if Indian biological resources/associated TK are used in the invention.
2. Check if NBA or State Board approval is needed for your category.
3. File ABS application and obtain approval before IPR filing/grant as required.
4. Disclose source in patent application and comply with benefit■sharing terms.
Biological Diversity Act 2002 (as amended 2023); Biological Diversity Rules 2004 (as amended); Patents Act 1970
(source disclosure requirements).
National Biodiversity Authority; State Biodiversity Boards; Indian Patent Office.
Common Mistakes
 Filing patent without checking ABS obligations.
 Incorrect or incomplete source disclosure in patent application.
 Assuming ABS only applies to foreign entities.
Isolating an active compound from an Indian herb and filing a patent triggers ABS obligations; applicant must disclose
source and show NBA/State Board compliance.
 Source title: NBA portal; PIB notifications; Patents Act
 Issuing authority: NBA; MoEFCC; IP India
 URL: https://nbaindia.nic.in/ ; https://ipindia.gov.in/

ABS obligations depend on resource type, user category, and state rules; seek specific advice for complex cases.

5. Step-by-Step ABS Process for Ayurveda / Biological
Resource Users
What is a step-by-step ABS process for Ayurveda / biological resource
users?
Simple Answer
A typical ABS process involves identifying resources/TK, determining approval needs, filing applications with
NBA/State Board, negotiating ABS agreements, and complying with terms throughout the
project/commercialization.
For Ayurveda companies, this often intersects with product development, IPR strategy, and regulatory filings.
 Early ABS assessment avoids delays later (e.g., during IPR or product launch).
 Documentation of source, collection, and benefit sharing is critical.
 Coordination between R&D;, legal, and regulatory teams is essential.
Step-by-Step Process
1. Map all biological resources and associated TK used (herbs, extracts, formulations).
2. Determine origin (India/foreign), source (wild/cultivated/institution), and user category.
3. Check NBA/State Board approval requirements under current Act/Rules.
4. Prepare and file ABS application (e.g., Form■I) with required details.
5. Negotiate and sign ABS agreement specifying benefits (monetary/non■monetary).
6. Implement benefit sharing and maintain records for audits/compliance.
7. Disclose source in IPR applications where applicable.
Biological Diversity Act 2002 (as amended 2023); Biological Diversity Rules 2004 (as amended).
National Biodiversity Authority; State Biodiversity Boards.
Common Mistakes
 Starting R&D;/commercialization before ABS approval where required.
 Poor documentation of source and benefit sharing.
 Treating ABS as a one■time formality instead of ongoing compliance.
An Ayurvedic startup developing a new herbal formulation maps all herbs, confirms Indian origin, files ABS application
with State Board, signs ABS agreement with benefit sharing to local communities, and discloses source in patent
application.
 Source title: NBA portal; Biological Diversity Act/Rules
 Issuing authority: NBA; MoEFCC
 URL: https://nbaindia.nic.in/

Process details vary by state and case; always check latest official guidance.

6. Common Mistakes and Risk Areas
What are common ABS mistakes and risk areas?
Simple Answer
Common mistakes include ignoring ABS until late in the process, misclassifying resources, incomplete source
disclosure in IPR, and assuming exemptions without verification. These can lead to delays, oppositions, or
penalties.
Risk areas include new product development using Indian herbs, collaborations with foreign entities, and IPR
filings based on biological resources/TK.
 Late ABS assessment can delay IPR grant or product launch.
 Misclassification of resources (wild vs cultivated, Indian vs foreign) can lead to non■compliance.
 Incomplete source disclosure in patents can be used in oppositions.
 Ignoring state■level requirements and BMC roles.
Biological Diversity Act 2002 (as amended 2023); Biological Diversity Rules 2004 (as amended).
National Biodiversity Authority; State Biodiversity Boards.
Common Mistakes
 Assuming ABS only applies to large companies.
 Not updating ABS compliance when product scope changes.
 Failing to train R&D; teams on ABS requirements.
A company files a patent on an herbal extract without ABS approval; later, an opponent cites non■compliance with
Biological Diversity Act, complicating grant.
 Source title: NBA portal; Biological Diversity Act/Rules
 Issuing authority: NBA; MoEFCC
 URL: https://nbaindia.nic.in/
This is a general list; specific risks depend on your activities and resources.
