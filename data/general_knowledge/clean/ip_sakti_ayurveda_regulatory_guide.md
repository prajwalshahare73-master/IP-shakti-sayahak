1. How are Ayurvedic Products Regulated in India?
How are Ayurvedic products regulated in India?
Simple Answer
Ayurvedic products are regulated mainly as ASU (Ayurveda, Siddha, Unani) drugs under the Drugs &
Cosmetics Act/Rules and Ministry of AYUSH notifications. Some products may be regulated as cosmetics or
food/nutraceuticals depending on ingredients, claims, and intended use.
The regulatory pathway (licence, approvals, labeling) depends on whether the product is classified as a drug,
cosmetic, or food. This affects manufacturing requirements, permissible claims, and which authority you deal
with.
 ASU drugs: regulated by Ministry of AYUSH and State Licensing Authorities under Drugs & Cosmetics Act/Rules.
 Cosmetics: regulated under Drugs & Cosmetics Act/Rules (cosmetics provisions) and Cosmetics Rules.
 Food/nutraceuticals: regulated by FSSAI under Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006 and related regulations.
 Same herbal ingredient can fall under different categories depending on claims and formulation.
Drugs & Cosmetics Act 1940 & Rules 1945 (ASU drugs); Cosmetics Rules 2020; Food Safety and Standards Act 2006 &
Regulations; Ministry of AYUSH notifications.
Ministry of AYUSH; State Drug Control Authorities (ASU drugs); FSSAI (food/nutraceuticals).
Common Mistakes
 Assuming all herbal products are automatically ASU drugs.
 Making drug■like therapeutic claims for a cosmetic or food product.
 Ignoring state■specific licensing variations for ASU drugs.
A herbal hair oil with only cosmetic claims (hair care, conditioning) is regulated as a cosmetic; the same oil with
therapeutic claims (e.g., treats hair loss due to medical condition) may be treated as a drug.
 Source title: Ministry of AYUSH resources; Drugs & Cosmetics Act/Rules; FSSAI
 Issuing authority: Ministry of AYUSH; Ministry of Health & Family Welfare; FSSAI
 URL: https://ayush.gov.in/ ; https://fssai.gov.in/
Classification can be complex; borderline products need careful legal/regulatory review.

2. ASU Drugs vs Cosmetics vs Food/Nutraceuticals
What is the difference between ASU drugs, cosmetics, and
food/nutraceuticals for Ayurvedic products?
Simple Answer
ASU drugs are intended for diagnosis, treatment, mitigation, or prevention of disease; cosmetics are for
cleansing, beautifying, or altering appearance without affecting body structure/functions; food/nutraceuticals
are for nutrition, health support, or dietary supplementation without therapeutic drug claims.
The category determines which law applies, what claims you can make, what licence you need, and how you
label the product.
 ASU drugs: therapeutic claims; require ASU drug licence; regulated by AYUSH/State Drug Control.
 Cosmetics: external use for cleansing/beautifying; require cosmetic licence; regulated under Drugs & Cosmetics
(cosmetics) and Cosmetics Rules.
 Food/nutraceuticals: ingestion for nutrition/health support; require FSSAI licence/registration; regulated by FSSAI.
 Claims drive classification: therapeutic claims push product towards drug category.
Step-by-Step Process
1. List all ingredients and their sources.
2. Define intended use and claims (therapeutic vs cosmetic vs nutritional).
3. Map to ASU drug / cosmetic / food category.
4. Follow relevant licensing/approval pathway (AYUSH/State Drug Control/FSSAI).
Drugs & Cosmetics Act 1940 & Rules 1945; Cosmetics Rules 2020; Food Safety and Standards Act 2006 & Regulations.
Ministry of AYUSH; State Drug Control Authorities; FSSAI.
Common Mistakes
 Using disease■treatment claims on a cosmetic or food product.
 Filing for food licence while making drug■like claims.
 Not checking latest AYUSH/FSSAI guidelines on permissible claims.
An Ayurvedic tablet claiming to 'cure arthritis' is an ASU drug; a herbal tea claiming to 'support joint health' may be a
nutraceutical (subject to FSSAI rules and permissible claims).
 Source title: Ministry of AYUSH; FSSAI; Drugs & Cosmetics Act/Rules
 Issuing authority: Ministry of AYUSH; Ministry of Health & Family Welfare; FSSAI
 URL: https://ayush.gov.in/ ; https://fssai.gov.in/

Borderline cases (e.g., wellness products with implied therapeutic benefits) need expert regulatory advice.

3. Do I Need a Licence to Manufacture Ayurvedic Medicines?
Do I need a licence to manufacture Ayurvedic medicines in India?
Simple Answer
Yes. Manufacturing Ayurvedic (ASU) drugs requires a licence from the State Licensing Authority under the
Drugs & Cosmetics Act/Rules and applicable AYUSH notifications, along with compliance with GMP and
other requirements.
Licence requirements vary for classical vs proprietary medicines and by state. Some categories may also
require product permissions or approvals.
 Classical medicines: based on authoritative texts; specific schedule requirements.
 Proprietary medicines: new combinations/forms; may need additional scrutiny.
 GMP compliance and qualified technical staff are mandatory.
 Separate licences may be needed for manufacturing, loan licence, and wholesale/retail sale.
Step-by-Step Process
1. Determine product category (classical/proprietary).
2. Prepare manufacturing unit as per GMP and state requirements (premises, equipment, staff).
3. Apply to State Licensing Authority (AYUSH/Drug Control) with required forms, fees, and documents.
4. Undergo inspection; address deficiencies if any.
5. Obtain licence before commercial manufacture.
Drugs & Cosmetics Act 1940 & Rules 1945; AYUSH GMP notifications; State drug rules.
State Licensing Authorities (AYUSH/Drug Control); Ministry of AYUSH.
Forms
 State■specific application forms for ASU drug manufacturing licence (often Form 24■D/24■E or equivalents; check
state portal).
 Forms for loan licence, wholesale/retail licence as applicable.
Fees
 Licence fees vary by state and licence type; typically a few thousand INR for application and licence, plus inspection
fees.
 Not verified from current official source for all states; check respective State Licensing Authority portal.
Common Mistakes
 Starting manufacture before licence is granted.
 Non■compliance with GMP (premises, equipment, documentation).
 Incorrect product categorization (classical vs proprietary).

A company in Maharashtra sets up an ASU drug manufacturing unit for classical churnas and proprietary tablets, obtains
state ASU drug manufacturing licence after GMP inspection, and then starts production.

 Issuing authority: Ministry of AYUSH; State Governments
 URL: https://ayush.gov.in/ ; respective State Drug Control websites
State■specific variations exist; always check current state notifications, forms, and fees.

4. Classical vs Proprietary Ayurvedic Medicines
What is the difference between classical and proprietary Ayurvedic
medicines?
Simple Answer
Classical Ayurvedic medicines are formulations described in authoritative Ayurvedic texts (e.g., Charaka
Samhita, Ashtanga Hridaya) with specified ingredients and methods. Proprietary Ayurvedic medicines are
new combinations, forms, or presentations not fully described in classical texts.
The distinction affects licensing, labeling, and regulatory scrutiny. Proprietary medicines often face stricter
evaluation for safety and claims.
 Classical: well■known formulations from texts; generally easier to license if standards are met.
 Proprietary: new combinations/dosage forms; may require additional data (safety, rationale).
 Labeling requirements differ (e.g., reference to text for classical; specific disclosures for proprietary).
 Patentability considerations differ (classical formulations are often prior art/TK).
Drugs & Cosmetics Act 1940 & Rules 1945; Ministry of AYUSH notifications on ASU drugs.
State Licensing Authorities (AYUSH/Drug Control); Ministry of AYUSH.
Common Mistakes
 Treating a proprietary formulation as classical without textual basis.
 Omitting required text references on classical medicine labels.
 Assuming classical status avoids all regulatory scrutiny.
A tablet based exactly on a formulation in Ashtanga Hridaya for a specific condition is classical; a new combination of
herbs in capsule form for 'stress relief' not described in texts is proprietary.
 Source title: Ministry of AYUSH; Drugs & Cosmetics Rules (ASU drugs)
 Issuing authority: Ministry of AYUSH; Ministry of Health & Family Welfare
 URL: https://ayush.gov.in/
Authoritative texts and interpretations can be complex; consult AYUSH experts for borderline cases.

5. Labeling and Claims Basics for Ayurvedic Products
What are the basic labeling and claims rules for Ayurvedic products?
Simple Answer
Labels for Ayurvedic products must comply with the relevant law: Drugs & Cosmetics Rules (for ASU drugs
and cosmetics) or FSSAI regulations (for food/nutraceuticals). Claims must match the product category and
not be misleading.
Incorrect claims or labeling can lead to regulatory action, product seizure, or licence cancellation.
 ASU drugs: must include drug name, ingredients, indications, dosage, warnings, licence number, manufacturing details,
etc., as per Drugs & Cosmetics Rules and AYUSH guidelines.
 Cosmetics: must include ingredients (as per INCI or accepted nomenclature), manufacturing details, licence number,
warnings, etc.
 Food/nutraceuticals: must comply with FSSAI labeling regulations (vegetarian/non■vegetarian logo, nutritional info,
claims, FSSAI licence number, etc.).
 Therapeutic claims are only permissible for drugs; cosmetics and food must avoid disease■treatment language.
Drugs & Cosmetics Act 1940 & Rules 1945; Cosmetics Rules 2020; Food Safety and Standards Act 2006 & Packaging
and Labeling Regulations; Ministry of AYUSH labeling guidelines.
State Drug Control Authorities; FSSAI.
Common Mistakes
 Making disease■treatment claims on cosmetics or food labels.
 Omitting mandatory warnings or licence numbers.
 Using unapproved ingredient names or incorrect nomenclature.
An Ayurvedic oil labeled as 'cures eczema' without drug licence is non■compliant; the same oil with cosmetic claims like
'moisturizes and soothes dry scalp' may be compliant as a cosmetic if other requirements are met.
 Source title: Ministry of AYUSH; FSSAI; Drugs & Cosmetics Rules
 Issuing authority: Ministry of AYUSH; Ministry of Health & Family Welfare; FSSAI
 URL: https://ayush.gov.in/ ; https://fssai.gov.in/
Labeling rules are detailed and updated periodically; always check latest regulations and official templates.

6. IP and Regulatory Strategy for Ayurvedic Products
How do IP and regulatory strategies interact for Ayurvedic products?
Simple Answer
IP (patent, trademark, design, copyright) and regulatory (drug/cosmetic/food licence, claims) strategies must
be aligned. Regulatory classification affects what you can claim, which in turn affects branding (trademark)
and positioning. Patentability depends on whether the formulation is truly novel vs classical/TK.
A well■planned strategy uses IP to protect brand and innovation while ensuring regulatory compliance for
claims and licensing.
 Patents: only for truly novel/inventive formulations/processes; classical/TK formulations are generally not patentable.
 Trademarks: protect brand name/logo across relevant classes (e.g., Class 5 for medicines, Class 3 for cosmetics, Class
35/44 for services).
 Designs: protect unique packaging/bottle shapes.
 Regulatory: choose drug/cosmetic/food pathway based on ingredients and claims; ensure labels and claims are
compliant.
Step-by-Step Process
1. Identify what aspects are technical (patent), branding (trademark), visual (design), content (copyright).
2. Determine regulatory category (ASU drug/cosmetic/food) and licence needs.
3. Draft claims and labels that are compliant with the chosen category.
4. File trademark early; file patent only if invention is novel and non■obvious.
Patents Act 1970; Trade Marks Act 1999; Designs Act 2000; Copyright Act 1957; Drugs & Cosmetics Act 1940; FSS Act
2006.
IP India; Copyright Office; Ministry of AYUSH; State Drug Control; FSSAI.
Common Mistakes
 Filing a patent for a classical formulation already in texts/TKDL.
 Choosing a brand name that conflicts with existing trademarks in same class.
 Making drug claims for a cosmetic or food product to 'boost sales'.
A company develops a new standardized extract process (potentially patentable), brands it under a unique trademark,
uses distinctive packaging (design), and markets it as an ASU drug with compliant claims after obtaining licence.
 Source title: IP India; Ministry of AYUSH; FSSAI
 Issuing authority: DPIIT; Ministry of AYUSH; FSSAI
 URL: https://ipindia.gov.in/ ; https://ayush.gov.in/ ; https://fssai.gov.in/

Each product needs case■by■case analysis; this is a general framework.

Understand regulatory classification and claims strategy for a herbal hair oil.

3. Obtain appropriate licence (cosmetic or ASU drug).

Scenario 2: Ayurvedic Nutraceutical Supplement
User Question

Detected Intent
Understand whether product is food/nutraceutical or drug.
If the product is positioned as a dietary supplement with health■support claims (not disease treatment), it
may be regulated as a nutraceutical under FSSAI, not as a drug.
 Nutraceutical: health■support claims (e.g., 'supports immunity'), not disease treatment.

 FSSAI licence/registration required for nutraceuticals.
Step-by-Step Process
1. Define claims carefully (avoid disease■treatment language).
2. Check FSSAI regulations for nutraceuticals and permissible claims.
3. Obtain FSSAI licence/registration.
4. Ensure labeling complies with FSSAI rules.

Scenario 3: Ayurvedic Clinic + Product Line
User Question
I run an Ayurvedic clinic and want to sell my own line of oils and supplements. What licences do I need?
Detected Intent
Understand combined regulatory needs for services and products.
Clinic services and product sales are regulated differently. Services may fall under clinical establishment
rules; products need appropriate drug/cosmetic/food licences.
 Clinic: comply with Clinical Establishments Act (where applicable) and state rules.

 Trademark: protect clinic and product brand names.
Step-by-Step Process
1. Check state requirements for Ayurvedic clinic registration.
2. Classify each product (drug/cosmetic/food).
3. Obtain respective manufacturing/third■party licences.
4. File trademarks for clinic and product brands.
